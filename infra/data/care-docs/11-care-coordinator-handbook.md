# Care Coordinator Handbook (HB-001)

> **Fictional training document.** Synthetic content for a workshop. Not clinical guidance.

## HB-1 The coordinator's job in one sentence

HB-1.1 Make sure that every patient leaves hospital with the right appointments booked, the right
referrals sent, the medicines reconciled and the paperwork done — on time and without surprises.

## HB-2 Daily routine

HB-2.1 08:30 ward huddle: review estimated dates of discharge (DP-001 §DP-3.2).

HB-2.2 Open the care plan for each patient due to leave in the next 48 hours and work through the
pending tasks.

HB-2.3 Book follow-ups first — slots inside SLA windows go quickly (FU-001).

HB-2.4 Run medication interaction checks early so the pharmacist has time to review (MED-001).

HB-2.5 Check prior-authorization for every post-discharge procedure (PA-001).

## HB-3 Working with the digital assistant

HB-3.1 The Care Coordination Agent can look up patients, read care plans, list slots, book
follow-ups, create referrals, run the synthetic interaction check and answer policy questions with
citations.

HB-3.2 Policy and protocol questions are answered by the **Care Knowledge Agent**, which only uses
approved documents and always cites document and section IDs.

HB-3.3 You remain accountable. Read what the assistant proposes before you approve bookings or
referrals.

HB-3.4 If the assistant declines a request citing SAFE-001, route the question to the named role.

## HB-4 Worked example (fictional)

HB-4.1 Patient P-1042 (heart failure, HFrEF, Northwind Health Plan) is due for discharge.
The coordinator: (1) opens the care plan, (2) books a cardiology slot within 7 days of discharge
(FU-001 §FU-3), (3) creates any referrals listed in the care plan, (4) runs the interaction check
and routes the `high` spironolactone + lisinopril flag to the pharmacist (MED-001 §MED-4),
(5) checks whether an echocardiogram needs prior authorization with Northwind (PA-001 §PA-3:
`CARD-ECHO` not required, `CARD-MRI` required).

## HB-5 Common pitfalls

HB-5.1 Booking a slot on the right day in the wrong specialty.

HB-5.2 Assuming "no interaction flags" means "safe" (MED-001 §MED-4.2).

HB-5.3 Confusing "prior authorization not required" with "covered".

HB-5.4 Forgetting that a slot can be taken between listing and booking — handle booking conflicts
by choosing another slot in the window.
